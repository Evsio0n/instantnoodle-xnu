# Cellular (OnePlus 8: the SDX55 modem under iOS)

Status 2026-11-11, **verified on the phone** unless a line says otherwise: iOS shows the carrier and the signal, mobile data works beside
Wi-Fi, SMS goes both ways in Messages (Chinese, emoji, long messages, the lock screen's notification), and calls go both ways from iOS's own
Phone UI over VoLTE **with sound** (earpiece; the speaker button switches the amplifiers). Installed on the phone as the boot bundle
"Cellular 2026-11-11" (boot slot 1; "Metal 3 2026-11-06" stays in slot 0).

Not done or not verified: the level of a call (the DSP refuses a volume step without Android's calibration), the speaker and mute as a person
hears them, hold and call waiting, DTMF on the air, MMS, delivery reports, a long message of more than eight parts joined, a call over a
Bluetooth headset, Personal Hotspot (no row appears), emergency calls (not tried), a modem restart in the middle of a run with the IMS data
service, IPv6 for IMS, sleep with the modem up. With no proximity sensor yet the screen stays on at the ear. The redwood has no modem work here.

## How it works

**The modem** is an SDX55 on PCIe 2, spoken to over MHI: Sahara for its images and EFS, then QRTR, and QMI services on top (DMS, UIM, NAS, WDS,
WMS, VOICE, IMS settings, IMSA, PDC, DSD, LOC). The kernel's thread brings it online, registers, starts the data session and keeps
`kern.hackios.cellular_*` and `kern.hackios.modem_*` true. The modem is also a *client* of services this side must host, as Android does: a
TFTP server for its files, the IPA's handshake, and **IMS data (QMI service 0x302)**: without it the modem's IMS brings its "ims" network up
and gives it up again every 5 s and never registers. With it (`cell_dcm`, on with `cell=1`): the modem asks for the "ims" network, this side
starts it on a WDS client of its own and reports the address back (the indication needs TLV 0x12 with the request's own value, or the modem
drops it silently), and IMS registers about 2 s later. SMS and calls then go over IMS.

**iOS** talks to CommCenter, which cannot run here (it needs Apple's baseband drivers). `hk_commcenter` takes its two Mach services and
answers them from the kernel's sysctls: signal, registration, data, the carrier's name; SMS (it reads `kern.hackios.modem_sms_ring` and tells
imagent, and takes what Messages sends); and it is the **CallKit provider** callservicesd takes for the telephony one (bundle identifier
`com.apple.coretelephony` in its `__info_plist`, entitlements `application-identifier` and `com.apple.callkit`). CallKit's actions become
`dial`, `answer`, `end`, `dtmf` requests to the kernel; the modem's All Call Status indications come back as events.

**A call's sound** does not pass through iOS: the audio DSP and the modem exchange it between themselves. This side only sets that up
(`voice=3`, the default with `cell=1`): on the DSP's GLINK channel `mhi_sat` it tells the DSP where the modem is and does the mappings and
channel starts the DSP asks for (Android's `mhi_satellite`); it maps a 128 KB mailbox into both the modem's and the DSP's SMMU windows; and
while a call is up (`kern.hackios.voice_call`, written by `hk_commcenter` from the other end's ringing or the answer on) it creates the DSP's
MVM/CVS/CVP sessions between the microphone's port and the speakers'. `kern.hackios.voice_speaker` chooses earpiece or speakers,
`voice_mute` the microphone. `libhackios-call.dylib` gives callservicesd a receiver and a speaker route for a call and writes the switch
when the user picks one.

## Things that cost time (so that nobody pays twice)

- **Messages' BlastDoor service refuses to run unsandboxed**: the kernel's sandbox stand-in answers "sandboxed" to a BlastDoor service asking
  about itself (`bsd/kern/kern_minimac.c`).
- **No notification for an SMS**: IMDPersistenceAgent says "We don't post notifications during system migration" until the preference
  `kIMDPersistenceLastBuild` (`com.apple.imdsmsrecordstore`, user mobile) is this build. `hk_commcenter` calls IMDPersistence's own
  `IMDSystemMigrationSetHasMigrated(true)` at its start.
- **A kept `/var` says "no baseband"**: MobileGestalt keeps its answers on the data volume. A daemon cannot fix that after iOS has started
  (the helper writes the old answers out again); `hk_commcenter` marks `init`'s stamp (`/private/var/.hackios-camera`) and `init` renames the
  kept file before iOS starts at the next boot.
- **The modem's own debug messages print the numbers of a call, addresses and the IMS session's keys**: those files' lines are not printed
  (`diag_noise` in the driver). Do not widen the DIAG masks on a phone in use without looking at what they print.
- **Closing the kernel's log stream panicked**: see KNOWN_ISSUES K41. **The modem thread's stack overflowed** when a packet arrived while it
  waited for a QMI answer: its two 3-4 KB buffers are static now.

## Tools

`hk_commcenter qmi SERVICE MSGID [TLVHEX [PORT]]` (one QMI request by hand) · `sms DIGITS TEXT...` · `sms-status` · `call dial NUMBER | answer ID |
end ID | dtmf ID DIGITS | events | fake NUMBER | speaker 0|1 | mute 0|1` · `dcm HEX|again` · `diag HEX` · `imd-migrated` ·
`oslog SECONDS [NAME...] [-m WORD]... [-x WORD]...` (iOS's log for the processes named; through the agent's `exec`, 15 s at most).
None of them prints a telephone number: lengths only.
